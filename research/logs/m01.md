## Topic gaps (what the official syllabus lacked, why it was added, where)

| Gap | Why it matters | Where added |
|---|---|---|
| A functional/legal definition of an "AI system" | Students meet the term in regulation and news; the academic definition alone does not explain what gets regulated | 1.1 (OECD + EU AI Act) |
| AI system vs AI model | Risk and regulation attach to the system in use, not the model alone | 1.1 (Modern Extension) |
| Automation vs augmentation | The syllabus lists applications but not the design choice that determines their social effect | 1.2 |
| Levels of evidence | Students cannot tell a press release from a field trial; this is the core of critical reading | 1.4 |
| Heterogeneous effects | Averages hide who gains and who does not; central for the economics module later | 1.4 |
| Target-variable choice as an ethical decision | Obermeyer's case shows bias entering before any modelling | 1.4 (bridges to Module 11) |
| Scaling laws and their limits | "Bigger is better" is the most common marketing claim students face | 1.5 |

## Modern extensions added

- **Foundation models** and **multimodal AI** (1.3): why the old branch boundaries blur.
- **AI system ≠ AI model** (1.1): the unit of regulation and risk.
- **Generative vs predictive AI** (1.2): a useful but non-absolute distinction.
- **Scaling laws, Chinchilla and the emergence debate** (1.5): a verified, balanced treatment.

## To verify / limitations

- The "AI effect" (that we stop calling a solved task intelligent) is presented as a widely reported observation in the literature, not as an empirically measured phenomenon.
- Arabic NLP difficulties (rich morphology, absent diacritics, dialect variation) are stated qualitatively; no specific benchmark numbers are claimed. Adding a measured comparison would require a verified Arabic benchmark source.
- The automation/augmentation scatter plot in `m01_automation_augmentation` is explicitly labelled as a conceptual illustration, not measured data.
- The evidence-level ladder in `m01_app_questions` is a teaching heuristic, labelled as such, not a formal standard such as GRADE.
