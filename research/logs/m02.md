# Research log — Module 2: تاريخ الذكاء الاصطناعي

## Research log

| Topic | Sources used | Key findings | Date verified |
|---|---|---|---|
| First neuron model | McCulloch & Pitts (1943), doi 10.1007/BF02478259 | Threshold unit over a weighted sum; networks of them compute logical functions. No learning: weights are set by hand. | 2026-09-27 |
| Turing's proposal | Turing (1950), doi 10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433 | Replaces "can machines think?" with the imitation game; answers the main objections himself. | 2026-09-26 |
| Birth of the term | Dartmouth proposal (1955), raysolomonoff.com PDF | Proposal dated 1955 by McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester and Shannon; workshop held in summer 1956. Core conjecture: every aspect of intelligence can be described precisely enough to be simulated. | 2026-09-27 |
| Early machine learning | Samuel (1959), doi 10.1147/rd.33.0210 | Checkers program improving with experience; among the first working ML systems. | 2026-09-27 |
| Perceptron | Rosenblatt (1958), doi 10.1037/h0042519 | Probabilistic model that **learns** its weights from examples. | 2026-09-27 |
| First winter | Lighthill Report (1973), chilton-computing.org.uk | Combinatorial explosion argument: lab results on toy problems do not scale. Led to UK funding cuts. | 2026-09-27 |
| Chinese Room | Searle (1980), doi 10.1017/S0140525X00005756 | Syntax does not yield semantics; a behavioural criterion cannot establish understanding. | 2026-09-26 |
| Expert systems | Lindsay, Buchanan, Feigenbaum & Lederberg (1993), doi 10.1016/0004-3702(93)90068-M | DENDRAL documented by its own creators as the first expert system for scientific hypothesis formation. | 2026-09-27 |
| Backpropagation | Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams (1986), doi 10.1038/323533a0 | Practical training of multi-layer networks; the title's key idea is **learning representations**. | 2026-09-27 |
| Convolutional nets in production | LeCun et al. (1998), doi 10.1109/5.726791 | Gradient-based learning applied to document recognition (cheque reading). | 2026-09-27 |
| ImageNet | Deng et al. (2009), doi 10.1109/CVPR.2009.5206848 | WordNet-structured, millions of labelled images; created a shared evaluation benchmark. | 2026-09-27 |
| AlexNet | Krizhevsky, Sutskever & Hinton (2012/2017), doi 10.1145/3065386 | Large error-rate drop with a deep CNN on GPUs; shifted the field within two to three years. | 2026-09-27 |
| Deep RL | Mnih et al. (2015), doi 10.1038/nature14236 | Atari games learned from pixels and reward alone. | 2026-09-27 |
| Self-play | Silver et al. (2018), doi 10.1126/science.aar6404 | AlphaZero masters chess, shogi and Go **without human game data**. | 2026-09-27 |
| Attention | Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio (arXiv:1409.0473) | Solves the fixed-vector bottleneck in neural machine translation; learns alignment and translation jointly. | 2026-09-27 |
| Transformer | Vaswani et al. (arXiv:1706.03762) | Drops recurrence for self-attention, enabling parallel training and therefore scale. | 2026-09-27 |
| Foundation models | Bommasani et al. (arXiv:2108.07258) | Names the category; emergence and homogenization as defining properties and risks. | 2026-09-26 |
| Deep Blue | IBM History page | 1997 match against Kasparov; search plus an expert-designed evaluation function on specialised hardware. | 2026-09-27 |

## To verify / limitations

- The `m02_winters` chart is a **schematic** of attention and funding cycles, explicitly labelled as illustrative; it is not measured funding data. Replacing it with a real funding series would need a verified quantitative source.
- MYCIN's rule count is given as an approximate range (about 450–600), which is how it is usually reported; no exact figure is claimed.
- Branching-factor figures for chess (about 35) and Go (about 250) are standard textbook estimates, presented as approximations.
- The claim that researchers avoided the term "AI" during the second winter is widely reported in histories of the field; it is presented qualitatively and not quantified.
