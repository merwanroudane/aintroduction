## Notes on what the module deliberately does not claim

- **No paper count for the leakage survey.** The arXiv abstract states 329 affected papers; figures cited
  elsewhere for the published *Patterns* version differ. Since the module cites the published article but the
  count could only be verified against the preprint, lecture 10.7 says "17 fields and **hundreds of papers**"
  and gives no single number. The 17 fields, the 8-type taxonomy, the model info sheets and the civil-war
  reproducibility result are all stated, because all four are stable across both versions.
- **No ILO exposure percentage.** The publication page fetched carries the qualitative conclusions but no
  headline share. Rather than quote a figure from a secondary summary, lecture 10.6 uses the ILO result only
  for its *direction* (augmentation over automation; clerical exposure; higher-income countries), which is
  exactly the point the lecture needs — that the primary source contradicts the usual headline.
- **No country-level figures for Algeria or the Maghreb.** No verified national estimate of AI exposure or
  productivity effect was available for the region. The module therefore frames the regional question as a
  **hypothesis** in lecture 10.6 ("a country with a mid-level skill base may gain relatively more — this is a
  hypothesis, not a result") and never as a finding.
- **No invented DOIs, product claims, or video links.** Module 10 self-study items are all journal articles
  reached through verified DOIs, plus one institutional report page. No vendor documentation is cited in this
  module, so no product-feature claims are made.
- **Lab data is synthetic and says so.** `m10_reg_bias` and `m10_ts_split` simulate from known
  data-generating processes and state what they simplify; `m10_text_index` uses six Arabic snippets written
  for teaching, captioned as such, with deliberately short dictionaries; `m10_leakage_hunt` uses four
  constructed study designs. None presents itself as real data.
