### Note on the comparison matrix (lecture 7.6)

The matrix records **categories of documented functionality only**. It deliberately does **not**:
- rank the tools,
- compare quality or performance,
- state model names, context-window sizes or prices (these change fastest and would date the course immediately),
- claim feature parity — the cells say "documented", not "identical".

Every cell is traceable to a help-centre page listed in `references.yml`, read on 2026-09-28. The lecture
teaches the **verification method** (specific question → official source → link + date + conditions →
"not documented" when absent) precisely so the matrix can be rebuilt by the reader when it ages.

## To verify / limitations

- **All product claims are dated 2026-09-28** and sourced to help-centre URLs. They will age; the lecture says so repeatedly and teaches re-verification.
- **No model names, context sizes or prices** are stated anywhere in the module, deliberately.
- **Arabic token ratios:** the lecture explicitly declines to quote a figure, and instructs students to measure with the tokenizer of the tool they use. No unverified ratio is claimed.
- **Lab data is synthetic:** tokenizer is an approximate teaching splitter (stated in a warning banner), logits are hand-set, attention weights are hand-set, and the RAG lab uses real TF-IDF over a small built-in corpus with a templated answer rather than a generated one. Each lab states this in the interface.
- **The "sparks"/emergence debates** are handled in Module 3; this module does not restate contested capability claims.
- Claims about *why* models hallucinate are attributed to the two surveys and phrased as the dominant explanation, not as settled mechanism.
