# Research log — Module 9: النماذج التوليدية للصور والصوت

## Research log

| Topic | Sources used | Key findings | Date verified |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diffusion, core formulation | Ho, Jain & Abbeel (arXiv:2006.11239) | Forward process as a fixed variance-preserving noising schedule that allows jumping directly to any step; training objective is predicting the added noise. This is the basis of the lecture's central pedagogical claim: the hard problem (generation) was replaced by a fully framed auxiliary problem (regression on noise). | 2026-09-28 |
| Diffusion vs GANs | Dhariwal & Nichol (arXiv:2105.05233) | A published comparison showing diffusion outperforming GANs on image synthesis. The lecture cites this as the reason the field shifted, and — deliberately — also records the axis on which GANs remained ahead (single-pass sampling speed), to teach that "better" without an axis is meaningless. | 2026-09-28 |
| Latent diffusion | Rombach et al. (arXiv:2112.10752) | Moving the diffusion process into a compressed latent representation cuts compute by orders of magnitude. The lecture draws the social consequence explicitly: this is what moved image generation from lab hardware onto ordinary hardware, and therefore what made the whole public debate possible. | 2026-09-28 |
| Joint text-image space | Radford et al. (arXiv:2103.00020) | Contrastive training over image–caption pairs produces a shared space where semantic proximity is geometric proximity. Used to explain how a text prompt can steer image generation at all. | 2026-09-28 |
| Text-to-image systems | Ramesh et al. (arXiv:2204.06125); Saharia et al. (arXiv:2205.11487) | Two full system architectures built over that shared space. Saharia et al. is cited for a specific practical finding used in lecture 9.3: strengthening the **text encoder** improves image quality more than scaling the image model. | 2026-09-28 |
| GAN origin | Goodfellow et al. (arXiv:1406.2661) | The adversarial framing, cited for the contrast with diffusion: the discriminator *is* the loss function, and because it is itself training, the correction signal oscillates — the structural source of training instability. | 2026-09-28 |
| Raw audio generation | van den Oord et al. (arXiv:1609.03499) | WaveNet, cited as the origin of high-quality neural audio generation. | 2026-09-28 |
| Speech recognition | Radford et al. (arXiv:2212.04356) | Large-scale weakly supervised multilingual ASR. The lecture uses this to make a point that matters directly to an Arabic-speaking researcher: performance varies sharply by language and by the amount and diversity of training audio, so **published error rates do not transfer to your data** — measure your own with a small gold reference. | 2026-09-28 |
| Music generation | Agostinelli et al. (arXiv:2301.11325) | Text-to-music generation. Self-study note directs students to the paper's own risks/considerations section rather than its results. | 2026-09-28 |
| Video generation | Singer et al. (arXiv:2209.14792) | Text-to-video. Used for the temporal-consistency point: video is not a sequence of independent images, because each frame must cohere with its neighbours. | 2026-09-28 |
| **Bias — amplification** | Bianchi et al. (arXiv:2211.03759) | Four findings reproduced in the lecture with care: (1) ordinary prompts mentioning traits, descriptors, occupations or **objects** produce stereotyped output; (2) stereotypes appear **whether or not** the prompt uses identity/demographic language; (3) user-side counter-stereotype prompting did not prevent them; (4) institutional guardrails did not prevent them. Finding (2) is presented as the most important, because most everyday use does not mention identity at all. | 2026-09-28 |
| **Bias — measurement** | Luccioni et al. (arXiv:2303.11408) | A measurement method comparing variation from identity markers against variation from professions, applied to three deployed systems. Key finding used in the lecture: outputs correlate with US labour demographics **yet consistently under-represent marginalised identities**, to differing extents. This is the evidence for the "amplification, not reflection" argument. | 2026-09-28 |
| Provenance standard | C2PA Technical Specification 2.1 (page fetched) | Signed manifests that travel with a file and record creation and edits. The lecture states the limits as prominently as the capability: provenance attests to a file's history, not to the truth of its content, and its **absence proves nothing**. | 2026-09-28 |
| Content Credentials | contentcredentials.org (page fetched) | The deployed consumer-facing form of the same standard. | 2026-09-28 |
| Watermarking | Google DeepMind SynthID (page fetched) | Imperceptible in-content marking, detectable by a matching tool. Cited as one layer among three, never as a solution on its own. | 2026-09-28 |
| **EU AI Act, Article 50** | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (EUR-Lex); AI Act Explorer article text read in browser | Article 50 transparency obligations, in force **2 August 2026**: (1) systems interacting directly with people must make that evident unless it is obvious; (2) providers of systems generating synthetic audio/image/video/text must mark outputs in a **machine-readable** format detectable as generated or manipulated, with technical solutions that are effective, interoperable, robust and reliable **as far as technically feasible**; (3) emotion-recognition and biometric-categorisation deployers must inform those exposed; (4) deployers of deep-fake image/audio/video content must **disclose** it, and text published to inform the public on matters of public interest likewise. Exceptions recorded in the lecture: evidently artistic/creative/satirical/fictional works (disclosure limited so as not to hamper the work), assistive standard-editing functions that do not substantially alter the input or its semantics, text that underwent human review or editorial control with a person holding editorial responsibility, and law-enforcement uses authorised by law. | 2026-09-28 |

### Note on what the module deliberately does not claim

- **No detector accuracy figures.** Published numbers for AI-image and AI-text detectors vary by tool,
  version and content type and would date immediately. The module instead states the *structural* limits
  and draws a normative conclusion (a detector score is not evidence), consistent with lecture 8.6.
- **No legal conclusions on copyright.** Litigation over training data and over the protectability of
  generated outputs is ongoing and differs by jurisdiction. The module teaches **four questions to ask
  before any use** and directs students to the binding text and to a specialist, rather than offering a
  summary that would be both unstable and out of scope.
- **No claim that any current model "can" or "cannot" do X.** Capability claims about a specific generation
  of models age fastest. Where a limitation is described (counting, in-image text, temporal consistency),
  the lecture gives the **structural reason** so the student can predict failure modes rather than memorise
  a list — and says explicitly that these improve.
- **No named-tool ranking.** The module title names DALL·E, Midjourney and Suno as the course card does,
  and the content teaches the mechanisms and methods that apply to all of them without comparing or
  recommending products.
