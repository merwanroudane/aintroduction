## Notes on what the module deliberately does not claim

- **No vendor data-retention or training-on-input claims.** This is the module's single most deliberate
  omission, and it is stated inside the lectures rather than hidden here. Vendor policies change without
  notice and faster than any syllabus, so 12.2 teaches the **six questions** and requires students to read
  the current official page themselves and **record the date they read it**. The three vendor help-centre
  pages are cited as *objects to practise on*, never as sources for a factual claim about what any tool does.
  Lecture 12.2 says explicitly that no answer about a specific tool appears anywhere in the course, and why.
- **No attack recipes.** Lecture 12.3's injected payload is written **descriptively** (a described instruction
  block, shown so students can see it) and the `m12_injection` lab's outcomes are **pre-written text**: nothing
  executes, nothing is sent, and no working bypass phrasing is provided. The instructor note tells teachers to
  redirect requests for specific phrasings into "what control makes this phrasing harmless?" Extraction
  techniques from the Carlini/Nasr line of work are likewise described by their *findings* only.
- **No legal advice and no characterisation of any national law.** Lecture 12.6 opens with an explicit scope
  statement: it is not legal advice, does not describe Algerian or any regional legislation, and leans on the
  EU text because it is the most detailed and the most widely imitated template. Where a real decision is at
  stake, it directs the reader to the applicable national text and the competent body. The "where there is no
  comprehensive statute" section names only categories of continuing obligation (general law, sectoral rules,
  institutional ethics, indirect obligations, professional responsibility) and **no specific instrument**.
- **No claim that the research exemption is an exemption.** 12.6 corrects the common belief directly: research
  flexibility is conditional on safeguards, does not touch minimisation or security, has a legal definition,
  and is separate from institutional ethics approval — which the lecture names as the student's *first* and
  sometimes *only* practical authority.
- **Labs are synthetic and say so.** `m12_reidentify` and the `m12_identifiability` diagram simulate populations
  with **independent** attributes and assumed category counts, and both captions state the direction of each
  simplification: independence **overstates** uniqueness while the skewed category weights used **understate**
  it, and studies on real populations with better methods find uniqueness **higher** at comparable attribute
  counts. `m12_membership` uses logistic regression and an attacker who observes the true loss — **stronger**
  than a realistic attacker needing shadow models — and the caption says the *trend* is the established
  finding, not the absolute values. Results are averaged over six draws because a single draw was noisy enough
  to appear non-monotonic, which would have taught the wrong lesson; the monotone relationship in overfitting
  was checked across a 5×5 grid of training sizes and feature counts before the defaults were fixed.
